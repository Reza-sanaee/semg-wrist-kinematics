Project Overview and Key Objectives
Bachelor’s degree project  |  Continuous wrist kinematics using sEMG
2
The principal objective is to develop a deep learning model for continuously estimating three wrist angles from surface electromyography (sEMG) signals.

The project estimates motion continuously rather than performing discrete classification.
Roll: inward and outward rotation
Pitch: upward and downward wrist flexion
Yaw: leftward and rightward wrist deviation
Dataset: NinaPro DB2, including movement data and muscle signals sampled at 2,000 Hz.

Slide 8
Biological Signal Variation and the Project’s Change of Approach
Bachelor’s degree project  |  Continuous wrist kinematics using sEMG
8
Phase 3 output under the Global Repetition Split protocol
Reason for proceeding to the final phase
Phase 3 demonstrated a scientific finding: covariate shift in sEMG signals across individuals is so substantial that constructing a fully general model that works for a new person without fine-tuning is practically impossible.

Overall solution: return to a subject-specific intra-subject approach in Phase 4, while constructing a fully continuous and physiological model. Discrete labels are converted into smooth biomechanical motions, with the aim of bringing the error close to zero.

Slide 9
Phase 4  Continuous and Physiological Kinematics
Bachelor’s degree project  |  Continuous wrist kinematics using sEMG
9
Objective: move beyond simple classification and obtain accurate, smooth motion estimates that resemble the actual biomechanics of the human hand.
Innovation 1  Target Label Smoothing
A human hand cannot change its angle instantaneously in a square-wave step.

The earlier networks produced high-frequency oscillations when attempting to predict step functions.

A Gaussian low-pass filter with sigma = 400 transformed the target values into continuous curves.

The neural network learned to predict gradual acceleration, reaching a peak, and a smooth stop.

Slide 10
Final Model Architecture and Its Rationale
Bachelor’s degree project  |  Continuous wrist kinematics using sEMG
10
Why combine CNN and LSTM
A standalone CNN does not capture the movement sequence. A standalone LSTM is computationally demanding and extracts features poorly from highly fluctuating raw signals.

The hybrid architecture combines the strengths of both approaches.
Input noise
GaussianNoise improves robustness to sensor noise.
1  Conv1D × 2 and L2 regularization
Extract high-frequency spatial features.
2  BatchNormalization and MaxPooling
Accelerate convergence and reduce dimensionality.
3  SpatialDropout1D  0.2
Prevent overfitting in spatial feature maps.
4  LSTM  64 units
Capture complex long-term temporal dependencies.
5  GlobalAveragePooling1D
Aggregate feature maps.
6  Dense  64 to 3
Provide linear regression for three motion axes.

Slide 11
Innovation 2  Gain Calibration for Amplitude Attenuation
Bachelor’s degree project  |  Continuous wrist kinematics using sEMG
11
Challenge
The MSE loss function causes conservative network behavior. The predictions are temporally aligned, but their amplitudes do not reach the absolute values of ±1.
Solution and outcome
A gain calibration factor was applied to the model output:
y_pred_calibrated = y_pred_smoothed × 1.35
The outputs were then clipped to the permitted range [−1, +1].

At the final joint angle, this technique produces a completely flat, stable plateau and prevents oscillation.
